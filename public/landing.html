Systemic Structural Fracture

The Public Health Paradox: Starving Clinics Next to Expiring Warehouses

In India's current medicine supply chain, information is siloed into disconnected bureaucracies. Every year, lives are lost to acute shortages at rural clinic dispensaries while tons of identical medicines are incinerated just miles away.

emergency_home

1. The Peripheral Stock-Out Crisis

When a seasonal epidemic strikes (such as monsoon dengue, leptospirosis, or viral encephalitis), frontline Primary Health Centres exhaust their baseline antibiotic and IV fluid quotas in 48 to 72 hours.

Tragic Consequence: Impoverished rural patients are forced to purchase basic fluids from unregulated private drugstores at inflated rates or succumb to preventable septic shocks.
delete_forever

2. The Central Expiry Crisis

State and district pharmaceutical warehouses operate on rigid periodic push-allocations. Batches arrive, sit in buffer bays, and quietly surpass their expiry dates without dynamic redistributive intervention.

CAG Audit Reality: State health audits repeatedly flag hundreds of crores worth of incinerated essential pharmaceuticals that expired unused merely 25 km away from stock-starved clinics.
cloud_off

3. The Disconnected Data Sinks

Epidemiologists log fever curves on IDSP portals. Meteorologists map rainfall and heatwaves at IMD. Frontline pharmacists write paper registers. Crucially, none of these data systems communicate.

The Missing Link: Procurement only discovers an emergency stock-out weeks after a mortality spike has made regional headlines and patients have suffered.
